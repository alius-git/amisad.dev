The promise

Nothing about the buyer leaves. Ever.

  • A buyer states a need once, in her own words — budget, timing, place, taste.
  • Matching happens in a sealed, attested environment in a network slice near her — created for the match, destroyed when it is done.
  • Only offers that fit come back. Silence when nothing does.
  • Every match settles value across four parties — and every promise is verifiable by an independent auditor.
This is the one slide of pure narrative. The next 25 minutes are the system doing it, live. No mock data in the responses — the ledgers you'll see verify are real hash chains.

Act I — The Buyer Loop
Act I · scene 1 · s001.fulfillment

The gift

  • Elena publishes a standing offer — her catalog, her words, her terms.
  • Maya states the need once. It travels as an opaque envelope; upstream services carry it, never read it.
  • The sealed environment matches, attests its lifecycle, destroys itself — and the offer closes automatically.
  • Elena ships to a delivery point. Maya sees delivered. Elena never sees Maya.
Watch for: the need request contains no name, no address, no identifier — and the match still lands.
Console: Elena → "Publish the standing offer". Switch to Maya → "Submit the gift need" — open the response, point at handle + match id, then open the call log entry and show the envelope: nothing personal in it. Switch to Elena → "Ship it", then "Confirm fulfilled → settled". Switch to Maya → "Check delivery" (status: delivered). ~3 min. If the response mentions degraded/fallback mode, the edge was unreachable — the flow and the privacy story are unchanged.

Act I · scene 1 · the receipt

Four parties paid, one hash chain

  • Fulfillment triggers the settlement: seller, network, platform, ad partners — computed once, recorded forever.
  • The settlement and attestation ledgers are append-only hash chains; the storage role cannot update or delete a row.
  • Anyone can ask the ledger to re-verify itself, any time.
Watch for: "verify" returns every chain intact — this is the same receipt the auditor will re-derive from raw dumps in the finale.
Console: Maya → "Proof: the receipt is a hash chain". 30 seconds. Mention it survives pod restarts and VM reboots (PostgreSQL-backed, database-enforced append-only) if the audience is technical.

Act I · scene 2 · s002.fitting

The dress

  • A considered purchase: sleeves, warm fabric, anything but dusty blue, fitting before Friday.
  • Five dresses exist; the shortlist returns only the one that fits everything.
  • Nothing commits until Maya books — her choice is the commitment.
  • Elena's board shows a slot and a day. An appointment, not a person.
Watch for: exactly two notifications, ever — the shortlist and the booking confirmation. Quiet by default.
Console: Elena → "Publish the dress rack" (five offers land in one click — name the four decoys). Switch to Maya → "Ask for the shortlist" (open the response: the dusty blue, the sleeveless, the late one, the out-of-region one are all absent) → "Book the Thursday fitting" → "Notifications: exactly two". ~2.5 min, keep it brisk. Skip the optional fulfil step unless ahead of schedule.

Act I · scene 3 · s003.silence

The kill switch

  • Consent is a ledger, not a checkbox — and it gates execution inside the matching cycle itself.
  • Maya pauses. A seller publishes a perfect offer for her open need…
  • …and nothing happens. No environment, no match, no notification. Silence.
  • She resumes — and the open need is served immediately. The whole history is on the chain.
Watch for: the empty notification list while paused, and "rematched" the moment she returns.
Console: Maya → "Sign up", "Open a need nothing fits yet", "Pause participation". Switch to Elena → "Publish a PERFECT decanter offer" — let it hang a beat. Switch to Maya → "…silence" (green check: empty list), then "Resume" (find "rematched" in the response), then "Consent history". ~3 min. This is the act's landing — pause on the empty list.

Act II · scene 5 · s006.mandate

The delegate

Watch for: Maya's activity trail — every delegated action visible to her, attributed to Pat under the recorded grant.

Act II · scene 6 · s007.inventory

The stockroom

  • Alex certifies a connector in a sandbox before touching anything real; Elena grants exactly three scopes.
  • The ERP is the truth: a counter sale zeroes the last unit, and the item silently leaves the matchable catalog.

Watch for: no buyer identity or need content ever crosses the integration surface — no such API exists to misuse.

Act III — Trust & Operations
Act III · scene 8 · s004.failover

The sovereign slice

  • Two regions, one policy: the restricted jurisdiction is pinned to its compliant region — the roomier one is excluded at allocation time.
  • Two injected isolation faults: each environment self-terminates before the envelope is opened. Attested, aborted, destroyed.
  • Automatic retry completes the match — same compliant placement, exactly one settlement.
  • The systemic pattern becomes a cross-party case for the platform operator — linked by environment id only.
Watch for: the unrestricted placement answers region-b; the sovereign one answers region-a. Policy is the only difference.
Console: Tom → "Register both regions", "Unrestricted placement → region-b" (roomier wins), "Pin sovereignty → region-a" (policy wins). Elena → "Publish the ceramics offer". Tom → "Arm two isolation faults" — announce the harness hat. Switch to Maya → "Submit the ceramics need" (abort, abort, clean match). Tom → "The incident queue" (two incidents; envs land in the notebook), "An aborted environment's attestation" (created→attested→aborted→destroyed), "Escalate". Switch to Priya → "The cross-party case". ~3.5 min.

Act III · scene 9 · s008.mediation

The dispute

  • Remember the Act I gift? It shows delivered — Maya says it never arrived.
  • Sam's case holds operational metadata only. Buyer identity has no path into it.
  • When metadata is not enough: a consented disclosure — one artifact, time-boxed, logged on the consent ledger.
  • The refund posts as compensating entries: history is never edited, the chains still verify, the net reflects the refund.
Watch for: after the grant expires, the access path itself is gone — HTTP 410. Support never became surveillance.
Console: Sam → "Open the case" (uses the Act I match from the notebook), "Request a consented disclosure". Switch to Maya → "Grant it" (20s countdown starts in the notebook). Switch to Sam → "Read exactly what was granted", "Post the refund" (original entries untouched, net 0). Talk through the countdown, then "After expiry: 410" — the amber 410 with a green check is the punchline. Sam closed a dispute about Maya without ever learning who Maya is.

Act III · scene 10 · s010.certification

The audit

  • Ingrid certifies everything you just watched — the matches, the aborts, the consents, the mandate, the disclosure, the refund.
  • Four dimensions: attestation continuity, residency, consent integrity, settlement conservation — recomputed from raw dumps, trusting no self-report.
  • We tamper with one record, live. Certification localizes the exact modification.
  • Her own access log proves she only ever read.
Watch for: "compliant by design" as a finding on the record — not a marketing line.
Console: switch to Ingrid → "Certify the evidence trail" (violations: 0 over the corpus this demo just created — say exactly that). Finish with "The auditor's own access log". ~2 min. This is the close — let the zero-violations result sit on screen.
